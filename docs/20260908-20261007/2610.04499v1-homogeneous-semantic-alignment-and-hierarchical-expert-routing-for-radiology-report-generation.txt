A B S T R A C T 

Radiology report generation (RRG) aims to convert medical images into diagnostic texts to assist in clinical decision-making and alleviate the workload of physicians. Although existing methods have made extensive progress in cross-modal interaction and the incorporation of external priors, the distribution shift of underlying representations and the undifferentiated rigid coupling of het-erogeneous information cause weak visual abnormality cues to be easily diluted by massive text priors and generation inertia during decoding. To overcome this bottleneck, inspired by cognitive science, we propose a novel two-stage Homogeneous Semantic Alignment and Hierarchical Expert 

Routing (HSA-HER) framework. First, the model introduces an explicit homogeneous distribution constraint in the underlying latent space to effectively eliminate the cross-modal distribution shift between visual and textual features, thereby extracting purified visual features as semantic anchors that accurately align with diseases. Second, for heterogeneous clinical evidence composed of visual features, local entities, and global retrievals, we design a hierarchical expert routing mechanism guided by these disease semantic anchors. This mechanism abandons the undifferentiated rigid coupling paradigm. Specifically, it dynamically activates expert networks to perform targeted mining and semantic reconstruction on multi-source evidence, and adaptively allocates fusion weights. Extensive experiments on three mainstream benchmark datasets demonstrate that HSA-HER achieves state-of-the-art performance, accurately depicting complex imaging details and key diagnostic information. 

## 1. Introduction 

Radiology report generation (RRG) is a critical task in medical artificial intelligence. It aims to automatically convert medical images into diagnostic texts, addressing the growing demand for image interpretation and alleviating radiologists’ workload. Efficient RRG frameworks optimize routine clinical workflows, accelerate patient screening, and assist in clinical decision-making. To achieve this goal, existing studies primarily adopt the encoder-decoder paradigm. Early works focused on se-quence fitting for long texts, but these methods are con-strained by surface-level vision-text mappings and lack deep pathological semantic understanding [8, 19]. To address this, recent studies introduce large language models [18], large-scale vision-language pre-training [40], and retrieval-augmented mechanisms [39], expanding feature representa-tions and prior contexts. Although improving report quality, they still face bottlenecks in cross-modal feature alignment and heterogeneous information scheduling. First, existing cross-modal interactions mostly rely on attention fusion or instance-level contrastive learning [3, 38]. This shal-low alignment fails to eliminate the inherent modality gap, leading to feature distribution shifts and hindering the ex-traction of pure visual features as disease-aligned semantic anchors. ## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Cross-modal feature alignment in RRG 

Radiology report generation (RRG) aims to translate medical images into accurate and coherent clinical texts. Unlike natural image captioning [36], RRG requires ex-tracting weak pathological features, generating lengthy clin-ical reports, and maintaining strict medical logic. Early works primarily addressed these challenges by improving the long-sequence modeling capability for medical texts. For instance, R2Gen [8] and CMCL [19] utilized relational 

E. Zhang et al.: Preprint submitted to Elsevier Page 2 of 14 Homogeneous Semantic Alignment and Hierarchical Expert Routing for Radiology Report Generation 

memory matrices and multimodal curriculum learning, re-spectively. However, these purely data-driven methods relied on surface-level image-text mappings, lacking an under-standing of deep pathological semantics. To deepen pathological semantic alignment, recent stud-ies have explored various cross-modal interaction strate-gies. Early methods optimized image-text mappings via weakly supervised contrastive learning [50] and reinforce-ment learning [27]. Subsequent works explicitly guided visual-textual alignment using class activation maps for attention supervision [38] or concept-level matching via up-datable medical knowledge bases [52]. With the emergence of large language models (LLMs) and causal inference, cross-modal correspondence is now steered by diagnostic prompts [13] or decoupled from confounding factors via causal interventions [6]. To further refine this alignment, recent models employ large-scale contrastive pre-training [40], collaborative fusion networks for fine-grained lesion matching [11], and hybrid contrastive strategies to capture temporal semantics in longitudinal data [3]. Although the aforementioned works have significantly enriched cross-modal alignment methods, they mostly focus on reducing the relative distance between instance samples or rely on attention-based feature fusion. These strategies fail to effectively eliminate the inherent modality gap, resulting in a persistent distribution shift within the image-text latent space. Therefore, these methods struggle to extract purified features as semantic anchors accurately aligned with dis-eases, leaving the model without a solid diagnostic foun-dation when subsequently processing heterogeneous clinical evidence. 

2.2. Heterogeneous Evidence Scheduling 

To enrich diagnostic details in reports, recent studies have extensively introduced multi-source prior knowledge and heterogeneous clinical evidence. Early methods, such as PPKED [20] and GSKET [53], provided global guid-ance using medical knowledge graphs. To enhance semantic support and feature interactions, MSAT [44] incorporated an external medical concept network, while XProNet [37] learned cross-modal prototypes. To further improve evi-dence aggregation, recent architectures utilize tree-graph reasoning [10], expert token mechanisms [42], and dual-modality multi-scale visual feature flows [32]. To directly guide the decoding process, BootstrappingLLM [18] and DDaTR [31] employ retrieved instances and longitudinal information, respectively. To inject richer contextual infor-mation into LLMs, recent approaches introduce contextual residuals [39], construct associative memory modules via Hopfield networks [41], and utilize dual-modality similarity retrieval mechanisms [46]. Additionally, FedMRG [5] em-ploys dual adapters to handle heterogeneous reporting styles within a federated learning framework. Although these works have significantly expanded the prior contexts of models, they often lack dynamic and tar-geted semantic reconstruction for each type of heteroge-neous information when processing highly heterogeneous clinical evidence composed of visual features, local entities, and global retrievals. Furthermore, during the subsequent scheduling of multi-source evidence, these methods typ-ically adopt predefined aggregation paradigms, failing to adaptively allocate fusion weights based on the weakness of specific pathological features. This undifferentiated rigid coupling mechanism allows massive text priors and the inherent generation inertia of LLMs to dominate the autore-gressive decoding process. As a result, weak visual abnor-mality cues are diluted, which ultimately leads to templated report expressions and makes it difficult to accurately depict complex imaging details.

3.3. Stage 2: Hierarchical Expert Routing 

3.3.1. Heterogeneous Clinical Evidence Construction 

The established semantic anchors provide precise dis-ease guidance. Guided by these anchors, the model further integrates fine-grained anatomical morphology and clinical contexts for report generation. Before decoding, the frame-work injects priors and constructs evidence via two parallel branches. First, the global semantic anchor 𝒗 𝑎𝑛𝑐 is fed into the Stage 1 multi-label classifier. The resulting prediction prob-ability 𝒑 ∈ ℝ𝐶 is discretized into a structured diagnostic prompt sequence 𝑷 𝑐𝑙𝑠 . Bypassing low-level routing, this prompt is directly injected into the large language model’s conditional context as an explicit prior to establish a macro diagnostic tone. Second, the model constructs a heterogeneous clinical evidence set comprising three components. The visual fea-ture 𝑬 𝑣𝑖𝑠 ∈ ℝ𝑁 ×𝑑 is inherited directly from the dense se-mantic anchor ( 𝑬 𝑣𝑖𝑠 = 𝑽 𝑎𝑛𝑐 ). For each radiograph, MedKLIP provides representations of the spatial locations and presence states of 75 predefined medical entities, which are precomputed and stored as .npy files. These static embeddings provide lesion-level and anatomy-level cues. The global retrieval prior 

𝑬 𝑔𝑙𝑜𝑏 ∈ ℝ𝐿 𝑔𝑙𝑜𝑏 ×𝑑 provides a reference for global diagnostic semantics and report expression patterns with zero online search overhead. ## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Datasets 

We evaluate HSA-HER on three widely used public chest X-ray datasets, namely CheXpert Plus [4], IU X-Ray [9], and MIMIC-CXR [14]. The CheXpert Plus dataset comprises 223,228 chest ra-diographs paired with 187,711 anonymized clinical reports from 64,725 patients, featuring 14 chest pathology labels and RadGraph annotations [12]. The IU X-Ray dataset, provided by Indiana University, includes 7,470 frontal and lateral chest X-ray images associated with 3,955 diagnostic reports. The MIMIC-CXR dataset is the most extensive public dataset containing paired chest X-ray images and radiology reports, consisting of 377,110 images and 227,835 diag-nostic reports from 65,379 patients. For MIMIC-CXR, we strictly follow the standard split and preprocessing pipeline established by Chen et al. [8], resulting in 270,790 training, 2,130 validation, and 3,858 testing samples. 

4.2. Evaluation metrics 

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of our model, we employ natural language generation (NLG) met-rics, clinical efficacy (CE) metrics, and the GREEN diagnos-tic safety metric. For the NLG metrics, we report BLEU-1 to BLEU-4 [26], ROUGE-L [17], and CIDEr [35]. These metrics characterize the consistency between the generated texts and the ground-truth reports from complementary perspectives: BLEU measures modified 𝑛 -gram precision, ROUGE-L cap-tures sequence-level similarity based on the longest common subsequence, and CIDEr measures consensus using TF-IDF-weighted 𝑛 -grams. For the CE metrics, we follow the evaluation protocol adopted by Wang et al. [41]. We use CheXbert [30] to ex-tract clinical observation labels from both the predicted and ground-truth reports and compare their presence to assess diagnostic accuracy. Specifically, we report macro-averaged Precision, Recall, and F1 scores across the evaluated clinical observation categories. Finally, considering the unique characteristics of radiol-ogy reports, we further adopt the GREEN [24] metric to evaluate diagnostic safety. 4.3. Implementation details 

The visual encoder is instantiated as VMamba [21], and the input images are resized to a resolution of 224×224 . The model training was divided into two stages. In the first stage, Bio_ClinicalBERT [1] was employed as the text encoder. This stage was trained on four NVIDIA A40 GPUs with a batch size of 128 per GPU. In the second stage, the decoder backbone utilized Llama-2-7B-Chat [34] for the CheXpert Plus and MIMIC-CXR datasets, and Qwen1.5-1.8B-Chat [33] for the IU X-Ray dataset. Constrained by the memory overhead of the large language models, the second stage was conducted on a single A40 GPU, and the batch size was adjusted to 8. During this stage, the visual backbone was initialized with the weights from the first stage and updated via full-parameter fine-tuning, while the newly introduced hierarchical expert routing was trained jointly. Meanwhile, the parameters of the language model were strictly frozen to preserve its pre-trained language generation priors. 4.4. Comparison with state-of-the-art methods 

We comprehensively compare the proposed HSA-HER method with state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods for RRG. The comparative evaluations are conducted on the CheXpert Plus, IU X-Ray, and MIMIC-CXR datasets. The reported re-sults of the compared methods are collected either from their original publications or from two recent studies reporting comprehensive comparative results [41, 46], and all values are used as reported without any modification. 

Performance on CheXpert Plus. As shown in Table 1, HSA-HER demonstrates superior performance across both NLG and CE metrics. Regarding the NLG metrics, our model achieves SOTA results in BLEU-1 to BLEU-3 and ROUGE-L, reaching 0.383, 0.240, 0.161, and 0.283, re-spectively. More importantly, concerning the CE metrics that measure diagnostic accuracy, HSA-HER achieves the highest Recall (0.325) and F1 score (0.338). Furthermore, in the GREEN diagnostic safety evaluation shown in Table 3, HSA-HER obtains the highest average matched find-ings (1.789) and the optimal overall GREEN score (0.283). Specifically, HSA-HER effectively controls Type b and Type f errors, with their average counts reduced to the lowest levels of 2.099 and 0.073, respectively. These results indi-cate that while generating high-quality reports, HSA-HER effectively improves the coverage of key clinical findings and overall clinical accuracy. 

Performance on IU X-Ray and MIMIC-CXR. Table 2 reports the performance of the model on the IU X-Ray and MIMIC-CXR datasets. On IU X-Ray, HSA-HER achieves the best results in BLEU-1 to BLEU-3 and ROUGE-L. On the largest-scale MIMIC-CXR dataset, HSA-HER achieves SOTA results across all NLG metrics except ROUGE-L. Particularly for the CIDEr metric, which measures con-sensus using TF-IDF-weighted 𝑛 -grams, HSA-HER obtains the highest score of 0.248, while BLEU-4 reaches 0.135. Furthermore, in the GREEN diagnostic safety evaluation shown in Table 3, HSA-HER obtains the highest average matched findings (2.066) and the optimal overall GREEN score (0.323) on MIMIC-CXR, with the average counts of Type b and Type f errors reduced to the lowest levels of 2.317 and 0.102, respectively. Notably, Table 3 shows that HSA-HER has limitations in Type a, c, d, and e er-rors on CheXpert Plus and MIMIC-CXR. This may reflect the trade-off between improving abnormal finding cover-age and calibrating false positives, fine-grained localization, severity, and comparative descriptions, as well as noise and uncertainty propagation from multi-source priors. Overall, this advantage in language generation and clinical safety metrics further demonstrates the capability of HSA-HER to accurately characterize complex imaging details and key diagnostic information.

This closely approximates the actual diagnostic logic of clinicians while avoiding severe missed diagnoses. Overall, HSA-HER effectively prevents subtle visual clues from being diluted by text priors and generation inertia, enabling the generated reports to depict complex imaging details more accurately.

## 5. Discussion 

This study shows that, compared with methods such as WCL [50] and CAMANet [38], which mainly improved models through cross-modal alignment, and AM-MRG [41] and DADS [46], which expanded diagnostic context through memory or retrieval mechanisms, performance gains in RRG do not depend solely on attention fusion, instance-level con-trastive learning, or the introduction of clinical priors. More importantly, they depend on whether precise disease seman-tic anchors can be established and whether heterogeneous clinical evidence can be effectively scheduled under their guidance. Meanwhile, HSA-HER still shows limitations in Type a, c, d, and e errors under the GREEN evaluation, indicating that improved coverage of key clinical findings is not accompanied by simultaneous improvements in false-positive control, fine-grained localization, severity assess-ment, and comparative descriptions. Specifically, the current framework is more effective in controlling certain clinically significant errors, while limitations remain in the precise control of diagnostic attributes such as location, severity, and comparative relations. In addition, HSA-HER has mainly been evaluated on two-dimensional chest radiographs, and its fine-grained spatial reasoning for extremely rare lesions requires further investigation. Future work could extend the framework to three-dimensional medical images, such as CT and MRI, as well as longitudinal scans, to further evaluate its applicability to more complex medical imaging scenarios and model the dynamic evolution of diseases. 

## 6. Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose and evaluate a two-stage homo-geneous semantic alignment and hierarchical expert routing framework for RRG. By imposing an explicit homogeneous distribution constraint in the latent space to extract purified visual features as disease semantic anchors, and further using these anchors to guide a hierarchical expert rout-ing mechanism for the targeted reconstruction and adaptive weighting of heterogeneous clinical evidence, our frame-work effectively overcomes the limitation of traditional RRG paradigms in which weak visual abnormality cues are easily diluted by massive text priors and generation inertia. Experi-mental results demonstrate that our model achieves excellent performance across three mainstream benchmark datasets. This confirms the effectiveness of the proposed framework in mitigating report templating and accurately depicting complex imaging details and key diagnostic information.
