## SPEAR: A Spectral-Disentangled MoE Neural Operator with Knowledge-Guided Expert Aggregation for Large-Scale PDE Pretraining 

Dengdi Sun 1, Xiaoya Zhou 1, Xiao Wang 2*, Wanli Lyu 2, Jin Tang 2, Bin Luo 2

1. School of Artificial Intelligence, Anhui University, Hefei 230601, China 2. School of Computer Science and Technology, Anhui University, Hefei 230601, China 

Abstract 

Large-scale pre-training has improved the generalization of neural operators across diverse PDEs. However, existing PDE foundation models still struggle with heterogeneous dynamics, where shared representations may cause knowl-edge interference, while mixture-of-experts (MoE) architec-tures suffer from increasing expert redundancy. We propose SPEAR, a spectral-disentangled MoE neural operator with knowledge-guided expert aggregation for large-scale PDE pre-training. SPEAR decouples latent features into low- and high-frequency components, enabling shared modeling of transfer-able dynamics and specialized learning of PDE-specific pat-terns. To address expert redundancy, we design a knowledge-guided expert aggregation strategy that measures expert sim-ilarity from dataset-specific learned knowledge and routing preferences, enabling the identification and consolidation of similar experts. Experiments on twelve PDE datasets and multiple downstream benchmarks demonstrate superior per-formance in pre-training, fine-tuning, and transfer learning. Furthermore, our aggregation strategy reduces the number of experts by 50% while maintaining or improving prediction accuracy, achieving a balance between model efficiency and generalization for PDE foundation models. 

Introduction 

Neural operators have emerged as a compelling alternative for solving PDEs by learning mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces, achieving orders-of-magnitude speedups at inference time. However, existing neural opera-tors are typically trained under single-task settings—specific PDE families with fixed parameter ranges—resulting in limited generalization to unseen equation types, parameter regimes, or boundary conditions. Inspired by large language models (Vaswani et al. 2017) and vision foundation models (Radford et al. 2021), recent efforts have introduced large-scale pre-training into the PDE domain to learn transfer-able physical representations. While pre-training enhances generalization, different PDEs exhibit substantially different underlying dynamics, and modeling multiple physical pro-cesses within a single shared parameter space may lead to knowledge conflicts and negative transfer. To scale up modeling capacity while maintaining inference efficiency, recent work has introduced Mixture-of-Experts

(MoE) architectures (Shazeer et al. 2017) into neural op-erator design (Wang et al. 2026; Sun et al. 2026). MoEs employ dynamic routing mechanisms that activate only a subset of expert subnetworks for each input, enabling mas-sive parameter scaling without proportionally increasing per-sample computation. This paradigm shows promise in han-dling PDE heterogeneity: different experts can specialize in different equation types, physical regimes, or numerical pat-terns. However, MoE-based neural operators still face the following two critical challenges. As shown in Figure 1, we summarize the motivation and overview of SPEAR. 

Challenge 1: Spectral Coupling of PDEs . Different PDE families differ drastically in their physical behaviors. For ex-ample, elliptic equations describe steady-state equilibrium, with high-frequency features manifesting as smooth local extrema; hyperbolic equations describe wave propagation, with high-frequency features manifesting as sharp discon-tinuities; parabolic equations describe dissipative processes, with high-frequency features manifesting as decaying spikes. These differences are most pronounced in high-frequency lo-cal patterns, while low-frequency dynamics exhibit broad similarity across equation types. However, Fourier-based neural operators such as AFNO (Guibas et al. 2021) couple all frequency components within shared parameters, forcing morphologically distinct high-frequency patterns to coexist and causing severe gradient interference. Existing remedies, including multi-scale operators (Liu et al. 2024) and fre-quency enhancement modules (Chen et al. 2025), have yet to break this spectral coupling constraint. Therefore, a mecha-nism that can separate shared dynamics from specific patterns according to frequency characteristics is urgently needed. 

Challenge 2: Expert Redundancy of MoE . While MoE architectures enhance model capacity through specialization, this comes at a cost: as model scale grows, parameter redun-dancy proliferates, with many experts learning functionally similar representations while inference overhead increases significantly. Existing expert merging methods (Zhang et al. 2026; Li et al. 2026, 2024; Chen et al. 2024a) primarily con-solidate similar experts based on routing statistics or expert outputs, rather than the task-specific knowledge semantics that experts truly acquire. This "behavior-based" rather than "knowledge-based" merging strategy lacks physical ground-ing. It is worth noting that these two challenges are not inde-pendent. A key fact connects them: low-frequency dynamics are broadly transferable across PDE types and can be effec-tively captured by a shared backbone, while high-frequency patterns are PDE-specific and demand expert specialization. This observation naturally suggests a two-stage strategy: first, decouple frequencies to separate sharable low-frequency knowledge from PDE-specific high-frequency patterns, as-signing the latter to a dedicated MoE module for expert-level specialization; then, consolidate the high-frequency experts to eliminate redundancy, ensuring that each preserved expert captures truly distinct physical knowledge. In other words: decouple to specialize, then aggregate to refine. Building on this insight, in this paper, we present a 

SP ectral-disentangled MoE neural operator with knowledge-guided Expert Agg Regation ( SPEAR ). Our framework in-troduces two tightly coupled innovations. For challenge 1 ,we propose a spectral-disentangled architecture that explic-itly partitions the frequency spectrum. The low-frequency branch employs AFNO to capture global evolution patterns, while the high-frequency branch extracts fine-grained local structures via high-pass filtering and models them through an MoE module composed of shared convolutional back-bones and LoRA adapters. Dynamic routing based on high-frequency statistics activates a part of experts to efficiently learn local dynamics across PDEs. For challenge 2 , we de-sign a knowledge-guided expert aggregation strategy that compresses and reorganizes functionally similar experts by incorporating physical correlations among PDE tasks and expert preference information. Rather than routing statistics, expert similarity is measured by the learned parameters for each PDE dataset. Then, a dataset-level knowledge matrix is constructed and fused via bilinear projection, yielding se-mantic similarities for expert merging. Our main contribu-tions are summarized as follows: 1) We design a spectral-disentangled architecture that ex-plicitly partitions the frequency spectrum: a shared back-bone captures low-frequency dynamics across all PDE types, while a dedicated MoE module handles PDE-specific high-frequency patterns. 2) We propose a knowledge-guided expert aggregation strategy that computes similarities among experts based on their acquired physical knowledge during training and rout-ing preference information, and merges them via bilinear projection and hierarchical clustering. 3) Extensive experiments on large-scale PDE pretraining datasets show that our method learns transferable represen-tations, achieving strong performance across cross-equation pretraining, fine-tuning, and generalization to unseen PDEs with superior parameter efficiency. 

Related Work 

Neural Operators for PDE Solving 

Neural operators learn mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces (Lu et al. 2021; Li et al. 2020). Various neural operators have been developed for PDE mod-eling. For example, DeepONet (Lu et al. 2021) adopts a branch–trunk architecture, while the Fourier Neural Oper-ator (FNO) (Li et al. 2020) models global dependencies in the Fourier domain. More recently, methods such as MPP (McCabe et al. 2023), DPOT (Hao et al. 2024), Po-seidon (Herde et al. 2024), OmniArch (Chen et al. 2024b), and UniSolver (Zhou et al. 2025) have explored pre-training on diverse PDE datasets, advancing PDE foundation models. However, most existing methods adopt a unified architecture. As PDE datasets become larger and more diverse, differences in physical laws and dynamics may cause knowledge inter-ference across heterogeneous tasks. Therefore, modeling di-verse physical knowledge remains an important challenge in large-scale PDE pre-training. 

Mixture-of-Experts Models 

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) expands model capacity through sparse expert activation and has been widely adopted in language and vision models. Representative works include Sparsely-Gated MoE (Shazeer et al. 2017), Switch Trans-former (Fedus, Zoph, and Shazeer 2022), and Mixtral (Jiang et al. 2024). By activating only a small subset of experts for each input, MoE increases model capacity with limited additional computation. Recently, MoE has also been intro-duced into neural operator models. Methods such as MoE-POT (Wang et al. 2026) and NESTOR (Sun et al. 2026) use specialized experts to capture diverse physical knowledge and alleviate interference across heterogeneous PDE tasks. However, some experts may learn similar physical repre-sentations, leading to knowledge redundancy and inefficient parameter utilization. Therefore, reducing redundant experts while preserving model performance remains an important challenge in MoE-based PDE modeling. Xt+1 Xt+1

Expert Merging 

Expert merging is an emerging compression technique that identifies experts with similar functions or representations and integrates them through parameter fusion. Represen-tative methods include SMoE (Li et al. 2024) and HC-SMoE (Chen et al. 2024a), which construct expert relation-ships from routing patterns or output features and then merge redundant experts. However, most existing methods lack ex-plicit modeling of task semantics and domain knowledge, making it difficult to accurately characterize the knowledge learned by different experts. In PDE modeling, MoE encourages functional specializa-tion, while knowledge overlap may still exist among some experts. Incorporating physical knowledge into expert rela-tionship modeling can therefore improve the identification of truly redundant experts and enable effective compres-sion while preserving expert diversity, which is important for building efficient and scalable PDE foundation models. 

The Proposed Approach 

We propose SPEAR, a spectral-disentangled MoE neural op-erator with knowledge-guided expert aggregation, as shown in Fig. 2. SPEAR decouples shared low-frequency dynam-ics from PDE-specific high-frequency patterns, where the MoE module enables expert specialization for local dynam-ics. Parameter-efficient design and knowledge-guided expert aggregation further reduce expert redundancy.

Experiments 

Datasets and Baselines 

We evaluate our method on pre-training, fine-tuning, alterna-tive data splits, unseen PDE transfer, model scaling, ablation studies, and expert merging. 

Datasets. We construct a mixed pre-training set of 12 PDE datasets with diverse equation types and parameter set-tings from FNO (Li et al. 2020), PDEBench (Takamoto et al. 2022), PDEArena (Gupta and Brandstetter 2022), and CFD-Bench (Luo, Chen, and Zhang 2023). 

Baselines. We compare against representative neural op-erators and PDE foundation models, including FNO (Li et al. 2020), UNet (Ronneberger, Fischer, and Brox 2015), GK-T (Cao 2021), GNOT (Hao et al. 2023), OFormer (Li, Meidani, and Farimani 2022), MPP (McCabe et al. 2023), DPOT (Hao et al. 2024), UniSolver (Zhou et al. 2025), MoE-POT (Wang et al. 2026), NESTOR (Sun et al. 2026), and Po-seidon (Herde et al. 2024). UNet, GK-T, GNOT, and OFormer are trained separately on each dataset, while the others follow their original pre-training and transfer settings. 

Main Results 

Table 1 compares our method across pre-training, fine-tuning, and expert merging. Overall, our method achieves the best performance on 8 of the 12 tasks. 

Pre-training Stage. Our method achieves the best per-formance on 7 tasks, including 5 of the 6 PDEBench tasks, while maintaining low L2RE on most others. This demon-strates that the proposed MoE architecture effectively learns shared physical knowledge across heterogeneous PDEs with high parameter efficiency. 

Fine-tuning Stage. We fine-tune the pre-trained model for 500 epochs on each downstream dataset. The resulting model achieves the best performance on 6 tasks. Compared with training from scratch, pre-training enables faster adaptation and higher prediction accuracy, indicating strong transfer-ability across PDE tasks. 

Expert Merging. We further compress the fine-tuned model by aggregating 12 non-shared experts into 6 accord-ing to their knowledge-guided similarities. Despite a 50% reduction in expert count, Ours-Merge maintains or im-proves performance on most tasks. For example, L2RE on PDEArena NS-cond decreases from 0.291 to 0.281, demon-strating nearly lossless compression. In contrast, OPCM (Tang et al. 2026) suffers substantial degradation as the number of merged experts increases, with L2RE increasing by tens or even hundreds of times on some tasks. This suggests that generic merging methods struggle to preserve functional differences among experts. Our method instead identifies redundant experts using cross-task knowl-edge and routing preferences, reducing redundancy while preserving expert diversity and critical PDE knowledge. Effectiveness Analysis of Expert Aggregation 

To evaluate the proposed aggregation strategy, we visualize the key matrices involved and compare downstream perfor-mance before and after aggregation. Figure 7(1) shows the dataset similarity matrix SD ,computed from ∆vi = vi − v0. Clear block structure is observed: datasets with similar physical mechanisms ex-hibit higher similarity, while different equation types show weaker correlations, indicating that the learned knowledge captures dataset-specific characteristics. Figure 7(2) presents the dataset–expert preference matrix R. The sparse and lo-cally concentrated pattern indicates that different datasets activate distinct experts, confirming that routing promotes expert specialization. The knowledge-guided expert similar-ity matrix SE , obtained by combining SD and R, is shown in Figure 7(3). High-similarity regions reflect knowledge over-lap among experts, guiding the aggregation of redundant experts while preserving essential knowledge. As shown in Table 1, reducing the number of experts from 12 to 6 achieves 50% compression with negligible performance loss, and even improves performance on certain tasks (e.g., PDEArena NS-cond L2RE decreases from 0.291 to 0.281). Overall, the strategy reduces parameter redundancy and improves representation consistency by merging experts with similar learned knowledge, balancing efficiency and gener-alization.

Ablation Studies 

We conduct ablation studies on six PDEBench subtasks to evaluate the contribution of each key component in the pro-posed framework. The results are reported in Table 5. 

High-Frequency Branch. Removing the high-frequency branch increases the average L2RE by 0.0152, causing the largest performance degradation among all variants. This confirms its critical importance in capturing fine-grained structures and sharp local variations. 

Mixture-of-Experts Architecture. Removing the MoE module increases the average L2RE by 0.0008, demonstrat-ing that dynamic expert routing effectively improves the mod-eling of heterogeneous PDE patterns. 

Load-Balancing Loss. Removing the auxiliary load-balancing loss increases the average L2RE by 0.0004. Al-though the impact is relatively modest, it promotes balanced expert utilization and stabilizes training. 

Orthogonality Loss. Removing the orthogonality loss in-creases the average L2RE by 0.0004, indicating that it en-courages diverse expert representations, reduces redundant feature learning, and promotes specialization among experts. 

Conclusion 

We presented SPEAR, a spectral-disentangled MoE neu-ral operator with knowledge-guided expert aggregation for large-scale PDE pre-training. SPEAR separates shared low-frequency dynamics from PDE-specific high-frequency pat-terns and reduces expert redundancy through knowledge-guided aggregation while preserving performance. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SPEAR achieves strong pre-training, transferability, and generalization to unseen PDE systems, providing an efficient and scalable solution for PDE foundation models.
